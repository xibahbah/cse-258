## pp. 1–27 · Factorization machines

Encode a row with sparse user, item, and context features. Add pairwise effects ⟨v_i,v_j⟩x_i x_j to a linear model. Shared factors let a rare feature pair borrow information from other observations.

## pp. 28–55 · Groups, multiple parties, and social signals

Group aggregation can favor average satisfaction or protect the least satisfied member. Reciprocal recommendations must account for both parties. Social links can regularize user representations.

## pp. 56–96 · Advertising and matching

With limited inventory, giving one user an item changes what remains for another. The bipartite matching examples introduce preferences, proposals, and stability.

## pp. 97–117 · Budgeted ad allocation

A bid alone ignores remaining budget and future queries. The balance algorithm and its weighted version trade immediate revenue against consuming scarce budget early.
